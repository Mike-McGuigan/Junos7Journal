Wine, Beer and the Route

A tasting list inspired by Juno's 7

A practical UK-friendly guide to bottles and beers that echo the style of the places Cameron and Sophie have passed through. Exact regional bottles are lovely when available, but a similar style is often the better supermarket choice.

How to use it

A supermarket-friendly tasting route

This is not a fixed price list. Supermarket stock changes constantly, so the guide is built around styles to look for, with a few useful UK starting points where they are available.

Match the place

Choose bottles that reflect the flavour of the voyage: crisp island whites, coastal Mediterranean wines, Maltese-style lagers, Sicilian seafood wines and volcanic Etna-style bottles.

Keep it relaxed

The aim is a family tasting table, not a sommelier exam. One good bottle per style is enough to make the route feel alive, even if the bottle comes from a neighbouring region.

Add beer

A few lagers in the fridge make the list easier for everyone: Mythos or Alfa for Greece, Cisk for Malta, and Messina, Moretti or Peroni for Italy.

Aegean IslandsActually from the route

Greece: Assyrtiko and island whites

Crisp, dry and mineral whites suit the early Greek island stretch through Rhodes, Chalki, Symi, Nisyros, Kos, Lipsi and Patmos.

  • Look for: Assyrtiko, Assyrtiko blends, Malagouzia blends.
  • Works with: grilled fish, prawns, lemon, olives and salty snacks.

Greece: crisp coastal whites

If Greek island whites are not available, choose a bright coastal white with citrus, salinity and clean acidity. These are the supermarket-friendly stand-ins for Assyrtiko.

  • Look for: Picpoul de Pinet, Albarino, Vermentino, Vinho Verde, Muscadet.
  • Works with: seafood, lemon, olives, grilled fish and salty snacks.

Dalmatian CoastActually from the route

Croatia: Posip, Malvazija and Plavac Mali

Croatian bottles can be harder to find in supermarkets, but specialist UK shops do carry wines that genuinely match the Dalmatian and Adriatic part of the route.

  • Look for: Posip, Malvazija Istarska, Grasevina, Plavac Mali.
  • Works with: seafood, grilled vegetables, lamb, salty cheeses and sunset anchorages.

Croatia: Adriatic-style seafood whites

When Croatian bottles are not easy to source, go for a dry coastal white with freshness, gentle herbs and enough acidity for seafood. Northern Italian whites are the easiest UK substitute.

  • Look for: Verdicchio, Soave, Lugana, Vermentino, dry Slovenian whites.
  • Works with: grilled fish, shellfish, risotto, courgettes and salty cheeses.

Grand Harbour and CominoActually from the route

Malta: Girgentina and Gellewza

Maltese wine is the most literal match for the Grand Harbour, Comino and Gozo section of the voyage, even if it usually needs a specialist or online search in the UK.

  • Look for: Girgentina, Gellewza, Maltese rose, Maltese white blends.
  • Works with: capers, olives, seafood, pastizzi and beach-day food.

Malta: sunny Mediterranean whites and rose

If Maltese bottles are not available, a crisp Sicilian white or dry Mediterranean rose captures the same sunny, island-table feel.

  • Look for: Grillo, Catarratto, dry rose, Vermentino, southern Italian whites.
  • Works with: snacks, seafood, capers, tomatoes and relaxed outdoor food.

Syracuse and Capo PasseroActually from the route

Sicily, Italy: Grillo, Catarratto and Nero d'Avola

Sicily gives the easiest supermarket options, with fresh southern Italian whites for seafood and fuller reds for tomato, grilled meat and richer pasta dishes.

  • Look for: Grillo, Catarratto, Inzolia, Frappato, Nero d'Avola.
  • Works with: calamari, shrimp ravioli, seafood pasta and tomato-rich Sicilian dishes.

Sicily, Italy: southern Italian seafood bottles

If a Sicilian bottle is missing from the shelf, choose another southern Italian white or soft red with ripe fruit and a coastal feel.

  • Look for: Fiano, Falanghina, Vermentino, Primitivo, Montepulciano d'Abruzzo.
  • Works with: seafood pasta, calamari, tomato sauce and grilled vegetables.

Riposto and TaorminaActually from the route

Etna, Sicily, Italy: Carricante, Etna Bianco and Etna Rosso

The volcanic stretch around Porto dell'Etna and Taormina deserves its own bottle, with Etna wines carrying the strongest sense of place.

  • Look for: Etna Bianco, Carricante, Nerello Mascalese, Etna Rosso.
  • Works with: swordfish, tuna, citrus, pistachio, aubergine and smoky grilled food.

Etna, Sicily, Italy: mineral volcanic alternatives

For the Etna mood without an Etna label, choose mineral whites or lighter reds that feel fresh, smoky and food-friendly.

  • Look for: Soave Classico, volcanic island whites, Loire Cabernet Franc, lighter Pinot Noir.
  • Works with: grilled fish, aubergine, mushrooms, citrus and pistachio.

Lipari, Panarea and StromboliActually from the route

Aeolian Islands, Sicily, Italy: Malvasia delle Lipari and island whites

The Aeolian Islands bring capers, seafood, volcanic slopes and sweet Malvasia. A true Malvasia delle Lipari would be the most direct match.

  • Look for: Malvasia delle Lipari, Zibibbo, Grillo, island white blends.
  • Works with: capers, olives, seafood, almond sweets and dessert.

Aeolian Islands, Sicily, Italy: aromatic island whites and dessert wines

If Aeolian bottles are hard to find, keep the island character with aromatic whites or a small dessert wine poured with something sweet.

  • Look for: Muscat, Zibibbo, Malvasia, Moscato d'Asti, off-dry Riesling.
  • Works with: almond biscuits, citrus desserts, capers, olives and salty snacks.

Beer option

A fridge version of the route

For a simpler version, build a cold beer shelf that follows the same geography.

Greece

Mythos or Alfa gives the early Aegean stops an easy, holiday-lager feel.

Croatia

Karlovacko or Ozujsko are the obvious Croatian names if you can find them through a specialist or online shop.

Malta

Cisk is the classic Maltese option and fits the Grand Harbour, Comino and Gozo section neatly.

Italy and Sicily

Messina, Birra Moretti or Peroni are easy choices for the Sicilian run, with Messina feeling especially appropriate if available.

Shopping basket

One practical version

  1. One Greek Assyrtiko, Assyrtiko blend, or similarly crisp Mediterranean white.
  2. One Croatian white if you can find Posip, Malvazija or Grasevina, plus one bright coastal white in the same style.
  3. One Sicilian Grillo or Catarratto.
  4. One Etna Bianco, Etna Rosso or Carricante-led bottle, or another mineral Italian alternative.
  5. One Nero d'Avola or Frappato for a softer red option.
  6. One sweet Malvasia, Zibibbo or aromatic dessert wine for the Aeolian Islands.
  7. A few beers: Mythos or Alfa, Cisk, and Messina, Moretti or Peroni.
